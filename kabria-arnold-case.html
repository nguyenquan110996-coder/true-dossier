KaBria Arnold Case: Nine Shots, a Silver Malibu, and a Life Sentence

True Dossier · November 19, 2026 · Case: KaBria Arnold

On the night of November 10, 2019, gunfire broke the quiet of a Detroit street and killed 20-year-old KaBria Arnold. She was pregnant. Police never recovered a murder weapon. So how did prosecutors persuade a jury, more than four years later, that Gabrielle Brantley was responsible?

Who she was

KaBria was an aspiring nurse and a softball player who had played for the University of Michigan-Dearborn. She also worked at a major supermarket in Detroit. Her family described her as warm-hearted and always ready to help the people she loved. She was the youngest of four daughters. After her death, her family heard from old coaches, T-ball coaches and even school principals.

She was pregnant, and her family only revealed that after she died. The medical examiner's findings showed nine gunshot wounds.

What happened

KaBria finished her shift and was on her way home. Officers found her at the intersection of Bentler and Pilgrim in Detroit. She died at the scene.

A witness inside a nearby house described what happened next. According to court records, a dark truck was parked outside the witness's home next to a silver Malibu. After the first shots, the Malibu drove around the corner onto Pilgrim. The shooter then got out, walked back to where KaBria lay, knelt over her and fired again. The witness could not identify the shooter's face and believed it was a man.

The investigation

Surveillance cameras captured a vehicle near the scene. Investigators then looked into KaBria's private relationships. Darnell Stevenson, the father of her unborn child, was also seeing another woman, Gabrielle Brantley. Police said Brantley had previously argued with KaBria over Stevenson.

Days after the shooting, police arrested a 28-year-old man, Stevenson, and a 24-year-old woman, Brantley. Stevenson was released without charges because phone records showed his phone was not near the scene. Brantley was charged with first-degree murder.

Across her interviews with police, Brantley gave conflicting accounts of where she was and which vehicle she used. At first she said she had never been near the scene, then her story moved step by step closer to it. Prosecutors also pointed to several pieces of evidence:

  • A silver Malibu registered to Brantley.
  • A prepaid TracFone bought days earlier, captured on store video. According to court records, it called KaBria three times that evening, and the paperwork was found inside the Malibu.
  • Online searches after the killing for the victim's name, for ballistics and for the Wayne County morgue.

The trial and outcome

The defense hit hard on what was missing. Police searched Brantley's home in Southfield and found no gun, no bullets and no detailed plan. A defense attorney argued the case rested on too little. The video notes that an empty search does not prove innocence, and that changing a story does not automatically make someone a killer. The question was whether the remaining evidence fit together.

At the preliminary hearing, the judge found probable cause, saying Brantley's "hands are all over this." The video stresses that probable cause is a much lower bar than a conviction.

At the end of 2023, a jury convicted Brantley of first-degree premeditated murder and of a separate charge for the death of KaBria's unborn child. She was acquitted of two firearm charges. On January 5, 2024, Judge Shannon Walker sentenced her to life in prison without the possibility of parole.

Brantley has maintained her innocence. She appealed, and on May 14, 2026, the Michigan Court of Appeals rejected her arguments and upheld the conviction.

Questions that remain

The sentence settled Brantley's legal responsibility. It did not bring back KaBria or her child. At sentencing, her sister Tiffany Arnold chose to speak about who KaBria was: her energy and her spirit. Her father, Kirk Arnold, urged anyone who knew who was responsible to speak up early, warning that someone who does it once will do it again.
