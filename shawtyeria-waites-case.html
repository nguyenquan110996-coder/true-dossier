Shawtyeria Waites: The Night Police Left Without Going Inside

True Dossier · 2026-11-19 · Case: Shawtyeria Waites

On the night of July 25, 2021, a neighbor in Houston called 911 about a woman screaming, as if desperately trying to get out of a door. Police eventually knocked, accepted a stranger's explanation and left. By then, investigators later concluded, Shawtyeria Waites had already been killed. The central question of this case is not only who killed her. It is what happened at that doorway.

Who she was

Shawtyeria had left her home state of New Mexico to attend Prairie View A&M University in Texas, a historically Black university. She was beginning an independent life, with plans of her own. July 25, 2021 was her 21st birthday.

What happened

According to court records, friends dropped Shawtyeria off at about 8:00 p.m. outside an apartment complex on Chimney Rock Road in Southwest Houston. She walked toward the apartment of Jordan Potts. A prosecutor's remarks quoted in the video suggested Potts lured young women with money; the video notes that these remarks raise questions but do not establish that he planned to murder her from the start.

At around 8:30 p.m., neighbors heard a woman screaming. A 911 call was placed, but patrol officers did not arrive until 9:48 p.m., more than an hour later. Potts answered the door and said the noise came from a video game. The officers did not go inside. They left.

In the parking lot, Shawtyeria's friend told those same officers she could not find her and that her calls were going to voicemail.

The investigation

Her family reported her missing on July 27. Their frustration grew as the days passed. On August 8, relatives, volunteers and community activist Quanell X searched the apartment complex and spoke with residents. Quanell X criticized the Houston Police Department publicly and said he believed the case would have received more urgency if she had been white. That is his opinion, not a finding.

The case was transferred to homicide on August 13, after investigators found evidence of foul play, 17 days after the missing-person report was filed. With help from the FBI, investigators analyzed cell phone data. It showed Potts's phone traveling west on the Katy Freeway in the early morning hours, then stopping in an isolated area near Brookshire, Texas.

On August 19, a warrant was granted to search Potts's car. Investigators reported finding blood in the trunk, a shovel and a gas can. On August 27, searchers recovered Shawtyeria's remains, along with the pink dress she was last seen wearing.

Houston police named Potts publicly on August 30 and announced a murder charge. He had left Texas and traveled more than 2,000 miles to Oregon. He was arrested in Gresham, Oregon, on September 1 and returned to Texas. His bond was set at $1 million.

The trial and outcome

A jury found Jordan Potts guilty of murdering Shawtyeria Waites and sentenced him to 45 years in prison. He appealed. On April 14, 2026, the Texas First Court of Appeals affirmed the judgment. His court-appointed attorney had filed an Anders brief, telling the court that, after reviewing the record, there were no arguable grounds for appeal.

Questions that remain

The video lays out three separate pieces of information: a report of screams, a friend's statement that Shawtyeria had gone to see Potts, and a family's missing-person report two days later. One location, one time frame, one missing woman. It asks whether they were compared in time.

Investigators concluded she was already dead when officers left, and that the departure gave the killer hours to move her body. Whether a different response that night could have changed the outcome cannot be known. The video notes there is no public record showing Houston police changed procedures because of her case. It points to the department's domestic abuse response team, launched in 2020, as a general example of how such calls are now handled.
