Alishah Pointer Case: An Unproven Suspicion and a Trial Cut Short

True Dossier · November 19, 2026 · Case: Alishah Pointer

On November 9, 2021, police found the body of a young woman in the basement of an abandoned house in East Cleveland, Ohio. Alishah Pointer was found on what should have been her 22nd birthday. She had been kidnapped, held, beaten and shot. And according to the police chief, nobody even knew whether she had the information her captors wanted.

Who she was

Alishah was 21 and lived in North Randall, Ohio. She came from a large family, and her closest bond was with her twin sister, Aliyah. Family videos show two sisters joking about what it means to be a twin. Relatives describe a kind young woman who worried about others. Since her death, Aliyah has marked her birthday without her.

What happened

Alishah was last seen on November 4, 2021, and reported missing the next day. Court records say the case began with a shooting in Cleveland on November 2 that killed a 19-year-old. People connected to him believed Alishah's boyfriend was responsible and that she knew where to find him.

East Cleveland Police Chief Scott Gardner said publicly: "We don't even know if the victim actually knew the information, but reportedly these people thought that she did." Nothing made public in the case has confirmed that she did.

According to court records, four people held two women at gunpoint and forced them into a car to show them where Alishah was staying. The group then took Alishah by force. Prosecutors say she was taken to a home in East Cleveland, where she was beaten repeatedly for hours. She was then driven to a burned-out, abandoned house on Savannah Avenue and shot there. The medical examiner determined she died from gunshot wounds.

The investigation

Police said six suspects were identified. By November 12, all but one, Hakeem Ali Shomo, had been arrested. He was later arrested in Hopkinsville, Kentucky. On May 31, 2022, a Cuyahoga County grand jury indicted all six defendants on charges including aggravated murder, murder, kidnapping, felonious assault and conspiracy.

Responsibility had to be weighed person by person. Under the law, someone who did not pull the trigger can still face severe penalties depending on their role, but not every charge applies to everyone.

The mistrial and the double jeopardy fight

In March 2023, Anthony Bryant, Brittany Smith and Hakeem Ali Shomo went on trial. On March 13, Judge John Russo declared a mistrial. A member of his staff had communicated privately with prosecutors about the evidence, without the defense. This is known as an ex parte communication. The judge also removed himself from the case.

Defense lawyers moved to dismiss the charges, citing the Fifth Amendment protection against double jeopardy. They were not disputing how Alishah died. They were challenging the state's power to try them again.

On May 31, 2023, Judge Janet Burnside denied the motion. She found the mistrial had been granted at the defendants' own request and that there was no evidence the former judge provoked it or intended it, so double jeopardy did not apply. An appeals court later upheld that ruling, and the three were sent back for a new trial.

Verdicts and sentences

Portia Williams, Destiny Henderson and Nathaniel Poke Jr. pleaded guilty in 2022 and 2023. In October 2025, Brittany Smith pleaded guilty. Juries convicted Anthony Bryant on October 31 and Hakeem Ali Shomo on November 25.

Sentences were handed down in January 2026, according to the Cuyahoga County Prosecutor's Office:

  • Hakeem Ali Shomo: life without parole, plus 36 to 41 and a half years.
  • Anthony Bryant: life without parole, plus 17 to 22 and a half years.
  • Brittany Smith: life, with no chance of parole for at least 41 years.
  • Portia Williams: life, with parole eligibility after 23 years.
  • Destiny Henderson: 25 to 30 years. According to the video, her gun did not fire.
  • Nathaniel Poke Jr.: 23 to 28 years.

Questions that remain

The procedural error forced a rebuild of the trial, but it did not erase the charges. The video's point is that a mistake can force a court to start over, while dismissing a murder charge requires entirely different grounds.

The deeper question has no legal answer. An unproven belief led six people to commit violence against a young woman who, as far as anyone has shown, may never have had the information they wanted. At sentencing, her family spoke of a loss that has no end. "Time does not heal," one relative said. "Grief doesn't end."
