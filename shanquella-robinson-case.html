"Four Years On, Who Answers for Shanquella Robinson's Death?"

True Dossier · November 22, 2026 · Case: Shanquella Robinson

Four years after Shanquella Robinson's death in Mexico, why has no one been convicted? An arrest warrant, two government investigations and a civil lawsuit have all come and gone, and her family is still waiting for a final answer.

Who She Was

Shanquella Robinson was 25, an entrepreneur from Charlotte, North Carolina, determined to build her business on her own terms. Her mother, Salamondra Robinson, remembers a strong-willed young woman who knew what she wanted and never backed down from her goals.

"She always said she wanted to have a successful business," her mother has said. "She could do anything that she said a man could do."

In late October 2022, Shanquella flew to Cabo, Mexico, with six friends to celebrate a birthday. The group rented a villa at the Fundadores Resort in San José del Cabo. Her mother says Shanquella was excited about the trip. They spoke on that Friday evening, and Shanquella mentioned that a chef was on site. It was the last time her mother heard her voice.

What Happened

On October 29, 2022, Shanquella died. Her mother says she received a call from Khalil Cook, a traveling companion she had considered a close friend. According to Salamondra Robinson, he said Shanquella was suffering from alcohol poisoning and that a doctor was on the way. She says she asked how he could know the diagnosis before a doctor had arrived.

She also says she was told a hospital required about $5,000 in cash. That is the mother's account of what she was told. It is not proof that a hospital made such a demand. Soon after, she says, the caller told her Shanquella had died.

The group returned to the United States. The family says they visited and insisted there had been no conflict. Then, Salamondra Robinson says, a stranger called to say there had been a fight in Mexico. Around that time, a video of an assault circulated on social media. The family and their attorney have said it showed Shanquella being attacked while unclothed.

The Investigation

A forensic report from Mexico stated that Shanquella died of a severe spinal cord injury and damage to the uppermost cervical vertebra. That contradicted the initial account of alcohol poisoning.

In November 2022, Mexican prosecutors began investigating her death as a possible femicide, the killing of a woman under Mexican law. The FBI's Charlotte field office opened its own investigation that same month. On November 23, 2022, Mexican prosecutors announced an arrest warrant for one of the travel companions, identified by the family and media as Dejanee Jackson. Prosecutors alleged she was the direct aggressor in the assault that led to Shanquella's death.

But a Mexican warrant cannot be enforced on U.S. soil. As one legal expert explained on television, no U.S. officer has the authority to arrest someone on a foreign warrant. Mexico's information must be vetted for probable cause and converted into a U.S. warrant, and an extradition request must clear a lengthy review.

The Decision and the Courts

On April 12, 2023, federal prosecutors announced they would not bring criminal charges. They said the available evidence did not meet the burden of proof beyond a reasonable doubt.

The cause of death was a central point of dispute. One report documented serious neck and spinal cord injuries. A second report did not confirm corresponding spinal injuries and left the cause of death undetermined. The family's attorney, Sue Ann Robinson, argued that Shanquella's body had already been embalmed before the U.S. autopsy, and that earlier action would have preserved evidence. Dr. Sherise Coston, a criminal justice professor at UNC Charlotte, cautioned that extraditing a U.S. citizen is a serious step that takes time, and that the U.S. autopsy finding of "undetermined" may complicate matters.

The family kept pushing. The hashtag Justice for Shanquella Robinson spread widely, and Kyrie Irving donated $65,000 to a GoFundMe page to help cover funeral costs. On October 29, 2024, the family filed a wrongful death lawsuit in Mecklenburg County Superior Court. It named the six travel companions, along with the FBI and the State Department.

In June 2025, a federal judge dismissed the claims against the FBI and the State Department. In January 2026, the remaining claims were sent back to North Carolina state court. On June 1, 2026, Judge Matthew Osman denied a motion to dismiss filed by Imani Green, formerly known as Dejanee Jackson. Green had argued that she was not properly served and that the statute of limitations had expired. She appealed on June 30. As of October 2026, that petition was pending before the North Carolina Court of Appeals.

Clearing a procedural hurdle is not the same as winning on the merits. No one has been found civilly liable, and no one has been convicted of a crime.

Questions That Remain

Is the obstacle gaps in the evidence, legal barriers between two countries, or delays that could have been avoided? The family's attorney has argued that Shanquella's race and status shaped how urgently the case was handled. Experts note that cross-border cases are slow by nature. Both views are part of this story.

Her mother asks the world to remember her daughter as she was. "She was loving, loved people, loved traveling," she said.
