Aniya Mack Case: A 25-to-40-Year Sentence and a Family's Anger

True Dossier · November 19, 2026 · Case: Aniya Mack

Donnovan Lewis pleaded guilty to murdering his girlfriend, Aniya Mack, and a Michigan judge sentenced him to 25 to 40 years in prison. By any legal measure, the case was closed. So why did Aniya's relatives walk out of that courtroom angry? The answer lies in the distance between what the law allows and what a family feels is fair.

Who she was

Aniya was 24 and a student at Western Michigan University in Kalamazoo, Michigan. According to her family, she was born and raised in Muskegon Heights. She was the middle sister, "loved by everybody," a daughter and sister who encouraged the people around her to do better.

After her death, friends held a memorial at her old high school. Her sister Ashley said Aniya would want people to celebrate rather than cry. Another relative said she cared about mental health awareness and autism research. Her family wants her remembered for how she lived, not only for how she died.

What happened

The last date recorded for Aniya before she lost contact was June 24, 2018. Her phone went unanswered. Her best friend, Thomas, told police it was completely unlike her to stay silent, and that if anything were wrong, she would have called him. By June 28, the worry had become a formal search.

Officers at her apartment noted details that did not add up. The bathtub was full of water and the air conditioner was running. A load of laundry had just been washed, including men's clothing. Her belongings were still inside. Her two Chevrolet Malibus were reportedly not roadworthy, and officers found that the license plate on one had been swapped onto another car.

At about 3:00 a.m. on June 28, Lewis approached the officers and told them he had killed her. He said she was "in the creek."

The investigation

Lewis led police to Comstock Creek, near Kalamazoo. Officers found a suitcase in the water. Inside was the body of Aniya Mack. Lewis was arrested and charged with open murder.

According to a confession quoted by local media from police records, Lewis said he knocked Aniya unconscious during an argument and then killed her with a sharp object. He then put her body in a suitcase and took it to the creek.

Lewis also offered a motive. A 79-page Kalamazoo Police Department report, reported by WWMT, said he claimed that messages on Aniya's phone made him believe she was involved with a local pastor. The same report noted Lewis had a history of assaulting Aniya.

These were Lewis's claims, not established facts. The pastor denied the allegations and said he would consult lawyers. The report indicated he had bought Aniya a phone and paid her rent at least once, and that the Malibu she used belonged to his wife. None of that proves what Lewis alleged. Aniya could not respond to any of it, and the person making the accusations was the person who had confessed to killing her.

While in custody awaiting trial, Lewis escaped. He was recaptured, but a family member said no one notified them and that he was missing for 33 minutes.

The plea and the sentence

Lewis's lawyer filed notice of a mental illness defense in August 2018. Aniya's mother, Loretta Bailey, rejected the idea, noting that Lewis had told police where to find her daughter. "It wasn't insanity," she said. "It was overkill."

Lewis ultimately pleaded guilty to second-degree murder. In 2019, a judge sentenced him to 25 to 40 years in prison.

The difference between first- and second-degree murder matters here. Under Michigan law, first-degree murder requires the prosecution to prove premeditation. Second-degree murder does not, though it can still carry a life sentence. Hiding a body shows an attempt to cover up a crime, but it does not by itself prove the killing was planned. The transcript also makes clear that, without a full record of the plea negotiations, we cannot say why prosecutors accepted that charge.

At sentencing, Aniya's mother's letter was read aloud. She wrote of the graduation, the wedding, the grandchildren and the Christmases that would never happen. Lewis apologized in court. Aniya's aunt, Linette Rankin, said she had not forgiven him. Lewis could one day be considered for release. Aniya will remain 24.

Questions that remain

Was the sentence proportionate to the crime? The law drew its line, and the court followed it. Many families in this position still feel that justice in the courtroom and justice as they experience it are not the same thing.

The case also left a warning. The YWCA in Kalamazoo used Aniya's story to help people recognize early signs of abuse, such as a partner who wants all of your time, objects to your friendships, or needs to know where you are at all times.
